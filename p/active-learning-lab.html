Target accuracy with 79% fewer labels.

Weak supervision bootstraps labels with zero manual annotation; active learning spends human labels where they matter — proven on a labels-vs-accuracy curve against random sampling.

Open the live demo → View code

Problem

The bottleneck in most ML isn't the model — it's labels. Hand-labeling is slow, and labeling random examples wastes budget on cases the model already gets right.

Approach

On SMS spam: 8 noisy labeling functions → a label-free label model (weak supervision), then active learning that queries the least-confident examples vs a random baseline.

Result

A learning curve that proves the saving — and a label model that down-weights its own bad heuristics without ever seeing a gold label.

Insights

What it shows

Smarter labeling beats more labeling. Uncertainty sampling keeps pulling near-boundary (often spam) examples, while random spends ~87% of its budget on easy ham.

Weak supervision degrades gracefully. A bad labeling function (11% accurate) was auto-down-weighted to 0.23 by a model that never saw a gold label.

For two classes, three strategies are one. Least-confidence, margin and entropy are all monotonic in |p−0.5|, so they pick identical points — a fact pinned by a unit test, not faked as "we tried three".
