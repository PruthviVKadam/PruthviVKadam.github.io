Causal ML · CATE · Live

Who to treat — not just whether treatment works.

Uplift meta-learners (S / T / DDR) on the Hillstrom campaign, scored by Qini rather than accuracy, behind a live targeting-policy simulator.

Open the live demo → View code

Problem

An A/B test tells you if a treatment works on average — not who to target, or who the "sleeping dogs" are that you hurt by contacting.

Approach

Train S/T/DDR uplift meta-learners on the Hillstrom email campaign, evaluate with Qini/AUUC, and expose a live targeting-policy slider (treat the top X% by predicted uplift).

Result

A targeting policy you can defend: spend on the persuadables, skip the lost causes and the sure things — measured against a random-targeting baseline.

Insights

What it shows

Targeting is a CATE problem, not a propensity one. Ranking by predicted incremental effect beats blasting everyone or chasing likely-buyers who'd convert anyway.

Qini is the honest scorer. Accuracy is meaningless for uplift (you never see both potential outcomes); the Qini curve measures incremental gain vs random.

Sleeping dogs are real. Some segments respond negatively to contact — a model that surfaces them protects revenue an A/B average would hide.
